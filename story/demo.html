Heat is not a citywide average.

Heat is a deadly, unequal hazard. With a hot summer coming and limited resources, cities need to know where help will matter most—and who is most at risk.

The hidden geography

Two blocks. One city. Different heat.

Shade and trees can make one street feel unlike a nearby stretch of asphalt. Citywide averages hide these local differences—and the people with fewer ways to find relief.

The decision gap

Three cooling centers. 102°F. Where should they go?

A city planner needs to choose locations with limited resources—and explain why those places, and whether people most at risk can reach help.

The planning question Urban Heat Planner takes on

  • Find local heat differences that citywide averages miss.
  • Compare candidate locations using heat and community context.
  • Make the recommendation and its trade-offs explainable.
